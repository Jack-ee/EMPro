#@10005 story 5: The Long Road Inland
the team had promised the driver an eta of nine o'clock, which everyone knew was optimistic.
gridlock outside the city cost them an hour, and then the truck's gearbox suffered a breakdown on the bridge.
while the mechanic worked, sofia spread the map across the bonnet.
their transect ran from the estuary inland, crossing a floodplain before climbing onto the plateau.
the topography changed so quickly that each kilometre demanded a different plan.
near the coast the soil was alluvial and soft, laid down by centuries of sediment from the river.
further up, a tributary cut through drained peatland where the ground was black and slowly sinking.
beyond that the land turned to laterite, red and hard, and the road climbed an escarpment in tight loops.
they stopped where a limestone outcrop marked the edge of the karst hills.
sofia walked the riparian strip along the water and counted seedlings while the light lasted.
below her the whole catchment lay open, undulating and green, with smoke rising from three separate fires.
they reached camp after dark, six hours late and too tired to argue about it.
dinner was rice and dried fish, unglamorous but filling, and nobody left anything on the plate.
#@10006 story 6: Fieldwork in the Monsoon
the workshop had been scheduled, unwisely, at the height of the monsoon.
every afternoon a downpour swallowed the road outside the hotel for twenty minutes.
between storms the air turned muggy, and a thin haze from distant fires blurred the hills.
daniel's counterpart at the local institute apologised for the weather as though he had arranged it.
nobody minded much, because the plantation visit was scenic in a way no report could capture.
they walked for three hours through a rubber monoculture that stretched to the ridge line.
it was sweltering under the canopy, and by noon two students showed early signs of dehydration.
daniel photographed leaf senescence along the older rows and noted where encroachment had pushed the boundary into forest.
fatigue caught up with everyone on the drive back, and the bus fell silent.
at the evening debrief they agreed on a single deliverable, a joint map to be finished by march.
one stakeholder asked for an embargo on the figures until the ministry had seen them.
the rest of the discussion concerned dissemination, since a map nobody reads changes nothing.
on the last morning his hosts gave him a small carved souvenir, a thoughtful gesture that made the whole trip memorable.
#@10007 story 7: Return to the Watershed
the itinerary gave them four days in the upper watershed, which nadia knew was two days too few.
she had spent her childhood in the next valley and still remembered the rhythm of the wet season there.
as a child she had picked longan and rambutan from trees her grandfather planted.
those trees were absent now, cleared for a plantation of areca catechu that ran to the river.
the forest above the village had not changed at all.
a leech found her ankle within ten minutes, and a tick had to be pulled off her collar at lunch.
her boot raised a blister by the second afternoon, and she remembered, too late, that her tetanus booster had expired.
the team's guide laughed and offered her cashew nuts from his pocket.
in the evenings they ate jackfruit and mangosteen on the veranda and argued about the data.
nadia had come to scrutinize forty years of land records for a retrospective study.
the verdict was not what the village expected, nor what she had hoped to write.
her old neighbours would read it as a judgement on themselves, and in part it was.
she sent the paper to a journal anyway, because the numbers were the numbers.
